Feature concept: One-click recipe and SKU replacement

Allow a user to replace any ingredient or prepared component throughout the system with one action:

SKU → SKU
SKU → recipe
Recipe → SKU
Recipe → recipe
Example use cases

A restaurant stops buying prepared pizza sauce and begins making it in-house:

Replace the purchased sauce SKU with the house sauce recipe across every recipe that uses it.

Or the reverse:

Replace an in-house dressing recipe with a commercially purchased dressing SKU.

Other common scenarios include:

Switching vendors or package sizes
Replacing a discontinued ingredient
Changing from house-made to purchased
Changing from purchased to house-made
Updating a standard prep recipe
Consolidating duplicate ingredients
Testing an alternative product across the menu
Why it is valuable

This is more than a convenience feature. It supports a premium positioning because it lets sophisticated kitchens adapt their entire costing structure quickly.

Without it, someone may need to open and modify dozens of recipes individually. With one-click replacement, FnB Cost Pro could immediately update:

Every affected recipe
Sub-recipes and nested recipe relationships
Ingredient costs
Plate costs
Theoretical food costs
Purchasing demand
Predictive order quantities
Vendor comparison opportunities

That last group is important because the replacement should not be treated as a recipe-only edit. It could change the restaurant’s entire purchasing forecast.

Important workflow controls

I would not make the button immediately destructive. A professional workflow would be:

Select the current SKU or recipe.
Select the replacement SKU or recipe.
Show how many recipes and locations will be affected.
Preview cost and quantity changes.
Confirm the replacement.
Record the change in an audit history.
Allow rollback if practical.

The preview might say:

Replacing “Sysco Alfredo Sauce, 4 × 1 gallon” with “House Alfredo Recipe” will update 14 recipes and reduce the calculated weekly ingredient cost by $186.

Questions to put to Jerry

Present the concept and ask:

How often does this happen in a real culinary operation?
Who should have authority to make a global replacement?
Should it update all locations or allow location-specific replacement?
Should historical recipe and cost records remain unchanged?
What happens when the replacement has a different yield, unit, or pack structure?
Would chefs expect a preview of every affected dish?
Should users be able to schedule a replacement for a future menu date?
Is rollback essential, or is an audit log enough?
Key technical warning

The hardest part is not replacing the reference. It is preserving the correct quantity.

For example:

One recipe uses 8 ounces of purchased sauce.
The replacement house recipe may be measured by a 1-gallon batch yield.
The system must convert the original 8-ounce requirement into the correct fraction of the replacement recipe.

Similarly, replacing one SKU with another SKU requires validation of:

Unit of measure
Pack size
Case quantity
Yield
Edible portion
Conversion factors
Location availability
Vendor assignment

A replacement should be blocked or flagged when the two items do not have compatible measurement geometry.
