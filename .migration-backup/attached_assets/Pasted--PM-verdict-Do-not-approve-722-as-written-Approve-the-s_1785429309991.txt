## PM verdict

**Do not approve #722 as written. Approve the simplified UI direction in #723 only after the underlying model is corrected.**

The proposed change solves a real usability problem, but it does so by moving a **vendor-specific purchasing concept onto the shared inventory item**. That creates serious problems for vendor comparison, multiple pack sizes, price history, ordering, and future imports.

The main issue is simple:

> A “case” is not a stable unit for an inventory item. It is a packaging configuration offered by a vendor.

A single ingredient may be purchased as:

* Vendor A: 1 case = 20 lb
* Vendor B: 1 case = 24 lb
* Vendor C: 4 × 5 lb
* Local vendor: sold by the pound

FnB Cost Pro already supports vendor comparison. Therefore, the inventory item cannot safely define one universal “case” as its costing anchor.

# The correct separation

## Inventory item

Represents the actual product or ingredient in a stable canonical unit:

* LB
* OZ
* GAL
* FL OZ
* EA
* L
* KG

Examples:

> Boneless chicken breast — canonical inventory unit: LB
> Eggs — canonical inventory unit: EA
> Olive oil — canonical inventory unit: FL OZ or L

This unit remains consistent regardless of vendor.

## Vendor item

Represents how a specific vendor sells it:

* Purchase unit: CS
* Pack: 4 × 5 LB
* Total canonical quantity: 20 LB
* Case price: $62.00
* Normalized cost: $3.10/LB

Another vendor item for the same ingredient can then be:

* Purchase unit: CS
* Pack: 2 × 12 LB
* Total canonical quantity: 24 LB
* Case price: $69.60
* Normalized cost: $2.90/LB

That is what makes valid vendor comparison possible.

## Recipe and issue conversions

Represent how the kitchen uses or counts the canonical inventory quantity:

* 1 LB = 16 OZ
* 1 bottle = 25.36 FL OZ
* 1 pan = 12 portions
* 1 bunch = 8 OZ usable product
* 1 recipe batch = 2.5 GAL

These relationships should not change because the primary vendor changes.

# Why #722 is dangerous

## 1. It makes the primary vendor define the inventory item

The migration says:

> Look up the primary vendor’s purchase unit and make it the inventory item’s unit.

That means changing the primary vendor could change the fundamental measurement anchor of the ingredient.

That is backwards. A vendor relationship should adapt to the inventory item—not redefine it.

Consider:

> Chicken breast is currently purchased from Sysco in a 20-pound case.

After migration:

* Inventory unit becomes `CS`
* One case is assumed to mean 20 pounds
* Recipe units become 320 ounces per case

Then the operator routes the order to a vendor selling a 40-pound case.

What does `CS` mean now?

* 20 pounds?
* 40 pounds?
* Whatever the primary vendor currently sells?
* Different things depending on which vendor price was last synced?

That ambiguity will eventually create cost drift or incorrect order quantities.

## 2. It conflicts directly with vendor price comparison

FnB Cost Pro needs to compare normalized prices across vendor-specific pack geometry.

The comparison should be:

> Vendor case price ÷ canonical quantity in that vendor’s case

It should not be:

> Inventory item’s current case cost versus another vendor’s case cost

Two cases are not necessarily comparable.

This proposal weakens one of the product’s existing premium capabilities.

## 3. `price_per_unit` becomes ambiguous

The ticket says:

> `price_per_unit` and `avg_cost_per_unit` represent cost per case.

Which case?

* Primary vendor case?
* Last received case?
* Cheapest vendor case?
* Weighted-average case?
* A manually configured standard case?

If the product has multiple vendor items, one item-level case price cannot accurately represent all of them.

A more reliable meaning is:

> `inventory_items.avg_cost_per_unit` = normalized cost per canonical inventory unit.

Vendor-item fields then store:

* Purchase price
* Purchase unit
* Pack geometry
* Canonical quantity per purchase unit
* Normalized price per canonical unit

## 4. It makes vendor synchronization destructive

The proposed sync updates the weight conversion row whenever vendor data changes.

That means a vendor catalog sync could alter the ingredient’s fundamental recipe-conversion geometry.

A price or pack change from a vendor should update that **vendor item**, not globally redefine the ingredient for all recipes, vendors, locations, and historical records.

## 5. Historical costing becomes unstable

Suppose an ingredient changed from a 20-pound case to a 24-pound case in July.

If the global inventory-item conversion changes from:

> 1 case = 20 lb

to:

> 1 case = 24 lb

historical recipes or purchase records referencing “case” may be reinterpreted using the new geometry unless every transaction stores a frozen conversion snapshot.

Canonical stock units avoid this problem.

## 6. “Case,” “Each,” and purchase unit are being conflated

The ticket describes the new anchor as:

> Purchase/case unit, e.g. CS, Case, Each

But these are not equivalent concepts.

* `CS` is a container.
* `EA` may be both the purchase and canonical unit for some items.
* A keg, bag, bottle, tub, tray, sleeve, or pail may be the purchase unit.
* A vendor may permit ordering by case and each.
* Some products are purchased by variable weight.

The architecture needs a general purchase-pack model, not a hard conceptual flip to “case.”

# Recommended model

Keep the stable inventory anchor and improve the user experience around it.

## Inventory item

```text
inventory_items
- canonical_unit_id
- average_cost_per_canonical_unit
- default_issue_unit_id, optional
```

You may retain `unit_id`, but its documented meaning should be:

> Canonical inventory or stock unit

Not purchase unit.

## Vendor item

```text
vendor_items
- vendor_id
- inventory_item_id
- purchase_unit_id
- purchase_pack_description
- purchase_price
- canonical_qty_per_purchase_unit
- normalized_price_per_canonical_unit
- inner_pack_count
- qty_per_inner_pack
- source_pack_text
```

Example:

```text
Purchase unit: CS
Pack: 4 × 5 LB
Canonical quantity per purchase unit: 20 LB
Purchase price: $62.00
Normalized price: $3.10/LB
```

## Inventory-item conversions

```text
inventory_item_units
- inventory_item_id
- unit_id
- canonical_qty_per_unit
- usage_type
- source
- active
```

Examples:

| Unit    | Canonical relationship | Usage     |
| ------- | ---------------------: | --------- |
| LB      |                   1 LB | Canonical |
| OZ      |              0.0625 LB | Recipe    |
| Portion |               0.375 LB | Issue     |
| Pan     |                   5 LB | Issue     |

Be explicit about direction. A field such as `canonical_qty_per_unit` is much less confusing than `qty_per_inventory_unit`.

For example:

> `canonical_qty_per_unit = 0.0625 LB per OZ`

or the inverse:

> `units_per_canonical_unit = 16 OZ per LB`

Either is valid, but the name must make the direction unmistakable.

# The existing field name is a liability

The ticket says:

> `qty_per_inventory_unit` means how many recipe units fit in one case.

Then later says:

> denominator changes from “lb per recipe unit” to “case per recipe unit.”

Those two descriptions conflict.

If the formula is:

```text
recipe-unit cost =
inventory-unit cost ÷ recipe units per inventory unit
```

then the field means:

> Recipe units per inventory unit

For example:

* $60 per case
* 320 ounces per case
* $60 ÷ 320 = $0.1875 per ounce

It does **not** mean “case per recipe unit.”

This semantic confusion alone is enough reason not to perform a major migration until the model is renamed or very clearly documented.

# Better direction for #723

The flat conversion table is a good usability improvement. The kitchen should not need to understand three tabs or database semantics.

But I would anchor the table to the canonical inventory unit—not automatically to a case.

## Suggested UI

### Purchasing section

Show vendor-specific purchasing packs separately:

| Vendor   | Purchase pack | Case price | Normalized cost |
| -------- | ------------- | ---------: | --------------: |
| Sysco    | 4 × 5 LB      |     $62.00 |        $3.10/LB |
| US Foods | 2 × 12 LB     |     $69.60 |        $2.90/LB |

This answers how the product is bought.

### Units & Measurements

Show one flat **Usage Conversions** table:

**Canonical inventory unit: LB**

| Kitchen unit | Relationship         | Purpose |
| ------------ | -------------------- | ------- |
| OZ           | 1 LB = 16 OZ         | Recipe  |
| 6 oz portion | 1 portion = 0.375 LB | Issue   |
| Pan          | 1 pan = 5 LB         | Issue   |

This answers how the product is measured and used.

The user does not need Pack Breakdown, Recipe Units, and Issue Units tabs. But purchasing packs and kitchen conversions should remain conceptually distinct.

# Do not hard-code one issue unit and five recipe units

The proposed limits are arbitrary:

* 1 issue unit
* 5 recipe units

A real kitchen may use the same ingredient as:

* Case
* Inner pack
* Pound
* Ounce
* Each
* Portion
* Pan
* Bag
* Bottle

Five may be sufficient for most items, but there is no clear domain reason to enforce that in the API.

A better KISS approach:

* Allow a modest total number of active conversions, perhaps 10.
* Let each conversion have one or more usage flags:

  * Recipe
  * Counting
  * Issuing
  * Purchasing display
* Show only relevant conversions in each workflow.

One conversion can serve both recipe and issue use. There is no need to duplicate “OZ” because it appears in two contexts.

If you retain the limits, they should be configurable or justified by an actual operational requirement—not by the current layout.

# Migration concerns

Even with a corrected design, migration must be much more conservative.

## Do not invent a case unit when no vendor exists

The proposal says:

> Fall back to “Case” or the existing container label.

That creates fictional packaging geometry.

If an item has no reliable vendor pack:

* Keep its current canonical unit.
* Flag purchase configuration as incomplete.
* Do not manufacture `1 case = ?`.
* Require vendor pack setup before purchase pricing is used.

## Do not select a primary vendor without deterministic rules

If multiple vendors exist, “primary vendor” needs an explicit definition:

* Marked preferred vendor
* Most recently purchased
* Highest purchase volume
* Current order-guide default
* Manually selected

Even then, its pack should only become the **default display pack**, not the inventory item’s identity.

## Migration must preserve history

If any values or semantics are changed, retain:

* Original unit
* Original price
* Original case size
* Migration calculation
* Selected vendor item
* Resulting normalized quantity
* Migration version

A parity test alone will not make the migration auditable.

## Test more than cost parity

Required tests should include:

1. Single vendor, simple case
2. Multiple vendors with different case sizes
3. Vendor change after migration
4. Each-purchased item
5. Variable-weight item
6. Inner-pack item
7. Item without a vendor
8. Item with missing case size
9. Recipe using ounces
10. Recipe using eaches
11. Purchase order quantity conversion
12. Predictive order conversion from canonical need to vendor cases
13. Historical receipt retains original pack geometry
14. Vendor price comparison remains accurate
15. No division by zero or null conversion

The current ticket explicitly excludes purchase orders and transfer orders, yet changing the unit anchor can affect both. That is a warning sign.

# Recommended product principle

The expert’s KISS instinct is correct for the user interface:

> A chef should see one understandable relationship instead of three technical tabs.

But KISS in the UI does not require collapsing distinct domain concepts in the database.

The safest principle is:

> **Store inventory in a stable product unit. Buy it through vendor-specific packs. Let the kitchen use whichever practical units it needs.**

For a chef, the experience can still be simple:

> “I buy this as a case of four five-pound bags, keep it in pounds, and use it in ounces.”

FnB Cost Pro should model that sentence directly.

## Final decision

* **Reject the backend flip in #722 as currently designed.**
* **Approve the flat-table usability objective in #723, but anchor it to the canonical inventory unit and show vendor purchase packs separately.**
* Require a full downstream impact audit before changing any unit-field semantics.
* Do not let a “primary vendor case” become the identity or costing anchor of a multi-vendor inventory item.

The proposed flip may make one screen easier today, but it would undermine vendor comparison and introduce ambiguity throughout the platform.
