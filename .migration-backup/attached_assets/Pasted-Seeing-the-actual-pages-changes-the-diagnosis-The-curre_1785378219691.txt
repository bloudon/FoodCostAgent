Seeing the actual pages changes the diagnosis. The current website is already moving toward photo-based onboarding, but the **entire commercial frame is still anchored to bargain hunting and a 14-day savings guarantee**.

That makes FnB Cost Pro look like a food-cost audit service with software attached—not a premium culinary operations platform.

## Marketing director verdict

The present site says:

> “Give us your data and we’ll find a savings opportunity.”

The new market position should say:

> **“Give your culinary team a faster, more natural way to create, cost, buy and operate—without sacrificing data quality.”**

Cost savings remains part of the proof, but it should no longer be the identity.

Your competitors already occupy the broad claims around controlling food cost, automating back-office work, mobile inventory, forecasting and vendor purchasing. Crunchtime emphasizes food-cost reduction and inventory accuracy; Restaurant365 connects purchasing, inventory, recipes and accounting; MarketMan promotes predictive ordering, vendor comparisons and live recipe costing; MarginEdge is strongly identified with phone-based invoice capture and reduced manual entry. ([Crunchtime][1])

Therefore, **“photo-based setup” alone is not enough to own the category.** Your stronger story is broader:

> **FnB Cost Pro lets chefs work naturally—from paper, photos, phones and existing files—while building the structured operational data needed by executive chefs, F&B directors and purchasing teams.**

That is where the premium differentiation lives.

# Homepage review

## 1. The hero is fighting the new positioning

Current headline:

> **Find Your First Food Cost Savings Opportunity in 14 Days**

Problems:

* It leads with a tactical financial outcome rather than the product’s identity.
* “First savings opportunity” sounds entry-level.
* “14 days” makes the product feel like a promotional challenge.
* The word **guarantee** appears repeatedly and gives the page a direct-response or lower-cost SaaS tone.
* It attracts restaurants looking to trim expenses rather than culinary leaders looking to elevate operations.
* It does not mention chefs, mobile use, recipe creation, predictive ordering, vendor comparison or multi-location operations.

The kitchen image is appropriate, but the copy over it says “food-cost consultant,” not “premium culinary intelligence.”

My strongest recommendation remains:

## 2. The statistic strip should be replaced

Current strip:

* 8 hrs weekly back-office data entry eliminated
* $5,000 value of a 1% food-cost improvement
* 14 days to a savings opportunity
* 1-day guided launch

This is heavily savings-oriented and feels like a sales funnel.

It also creates credibility issues unless every figure is well-supported and qualified. The dollar calculation may be mathematically reasonable for a $500,000 operation, but it does not demonstrate that FnB Cost Pro will produce that improvement.

Replace it with product truths:

| Current | Premium replacement            |
| ------- | ------------------------------ |
| 8 hrs   | **Photos to structured data**  |
| $5,000  | **Current recipe costs**       |
| 14 days | **Vendor pricing connected**   |
| 1 day   | **Predictive orders prepared** |

Or use a workflow strip:

> **Capture → Review → Cost → Compare → Predict**

This teaches the connected product story immediately.

# The guarantee section should be removed from the homepage

Current section:

> **The 14-Day Food Cost Opportunity Guarantee**

This section occupies prime space and reinforces the wrong buyer psychology.

Guarantees can work for lower-friction self-service software, but your current opportunity is consultative, operational and premium. A hotel, club, resort or chef-led group will not choose you because you guaranteed finding one questionable recipe or vendor increase.

They will choose you because:

* It can preserve complex data.

# The competitive whitespace

You should not claim competitors lack mobile access. Crunchtime, MarginEdge, MarketMan and Restaurant365 all promote mobile or streamlined operational workflows in various forms. MarginEdge especially makes phone-based invoice capture central to its marketing, while Crunchtime has mobile inventory tools. ([MarginEdge][2])

You also cannot differentiate solely on predictive ordering or vendor comparison, because MarketMan publicly offers predictive analytics, supplier price comparison and order management. ([MarketMan][3])

Your defensible marketing combination is:

1. **Chef-native capture across multiple input types**
2. **Structured, reviewable data rather than simplified guesses**
3. **Recipe creation and costing as a creative workflow**
4. **Vendor pricing and predictive purchasing connected to the recipe**
5. **Multi-outlet and physical storage-location awareness**
6. **Migration alongside existing restaurant systems**
7. **Premium guided implementation with culinary feedback**

That full combination is much stronger than any single feature claim.

# Pricing page concern

I have not seen the pricing screenshot yet, but the homepage’s trial/guarantee language suggests a self-service SaaS model.

Your pricing structure—base platform, additional locations and onboarding—can still work. However, for premium positioning I would rename and reframe it:

* **Platform Subscription**
* **Additional Operations or Locations**
* **Guided Implementation**
* **Enterprise and Multi-Outlet**
* **Vendor Connectivity**

Avoid leading with “cancel anytime” on the homepage. That is useful reassurance during checkout, but it weakens the enterprise and culinary-leadership tone in the hero.
