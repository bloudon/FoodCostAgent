Where the product defect actually sits

Not in matching — in what got offered. A row whose vendor, stable item code, and parsed geometry are byte-identical to an approved prior month has nothing to decide. It should match by code mapping and never enter Category 2 at all.

The question worth putting to Replit is narrower than "why was it accepted": why is a row with an unchanged stable code, unchanged vendor and unchanged pack presented as a new-pack-size decision? Ten of them carried the June item as comparableInventoryItemId, so the correct target was known at classification time.

The Crab Claw and Heitz cases sharpen it — both June and July independently forked against the same older base item. Two consecutive months producing new variants from unchanged evidence isn't a review slip, it's the classifier repeatedly offering a choice that shouldn't exist.

What I'd change in the procedure regardless

The runbook says verify the flagged rows. That's insufficient, twice over — it missed 5 cross-vendor cases that weren't flagged, and 12 identical rows that couldn't be flagged. The corrected rule:

Before confirming any Category 2 bulk, check every listed item against the prior month. Anything with an unchanged vendor + code + pack does not belong in the set. Anything with a changed vendor should be a link_vendor_pack, whether or not the dialog flags it.

For 30–100 items that's a workbook query, not manual inspection — I can generate the exclusion list mechanically before touching the bulk.
