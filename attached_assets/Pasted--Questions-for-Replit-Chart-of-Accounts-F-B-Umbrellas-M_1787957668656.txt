# Questions for Replit — Chart of Accounts, F&B Umbrellas, Multi-Tenant Structure

**Context:** Bay Hill's Orderly export contains a `GL Code` column that maps 1:1 to Category, and we have a 34-account chart of accounts exported from Orderly. Neither is currently captured by the FnB import. Before designing the adoption path, we need to understand what already exists in FnB's architecture.

**What we know from the data:**

* 25 of 26 GL codes used by inventory match the chart of accounts exactly (name and number). The exception is `999900 / No Account`, Orderly's unassigned placeholder.
* The chart carries two independent axes: `Financial Category` (Sales / COGS / Other Expense) and `Operational Type` (Food / Bar / Direct Operating Cost / Other).
* These do not nest. `502800 TOGO Supplies` is COGS financially but Direct Operating Cost operationally — the only DOC account classified as COGS.
* Revenue splits Bar into three accounts (Beer / Wine / Liquor Sales) while COGS splits it into seven categories, so revenue-to-cost joins have to happen at the umbrella level, not category level.
* Per Orderly's own export note: GL account is assigned per ingredient via a category dropdown; the ingredient has no separate GL field. Category → account is authoritative and uniform.

---

## A. What exists today

1. Is there any **accounts / chart-of-accounts table** in the schema, or any accounting integration that already models GL accounts? We found `categories.accountingAccountId` (varchar, unpopulated) and a `sourceGlCode` field around `schema.ts:2613` — what are each of those for, and is either already wired to anything?

2. `categories.accountingAccountId` has no foreign key. **Is it intended to reference an internal account record, or to hold an external system's identifier?** Its name suggests the former; its type suggests the latter.

3. Is there **any existing concept of a Food / Beverage / Other grouping** above category — in reporting, recipe costing, menu engineering, or the P&L — or would this be net new?

4. How do current reports that need a Food-vs-Bar split derive it today? By category name matching, a hardcoded list, or not at all?

---

## B. Multi-tenant boundaries

5. `categories` is `unique(companyId, name)`, so categories are tenant-scoped. **Should accounts be fully tenant-owned as well**, or is there an existing pattern for platform-provided reference data that tenants adopt or override?

6. Our working assumption: the **accounts** belong to the tenant (Bay Hill's `510800 = Wine` is Bay Hill's), while the **reporting structure** — Operational Type and Financial Category — is platform-defined so reports and any future benchmarking work across tenants. Does that match how FnB handles similar tenant-vs-platform splits elsewhere?

7. Is **cross-tenant benchmarking** on the roadmap — e.g. comparing a club's bar COGS % against peers? This materially changes the design. Platform-defined umbrellas make it possible; tenant-defined free text does not, and retrofitting later means re-mapping every tenant's chart.

8. If Operational Type is platform-defined, what is the right mechanism for a tenant who disagrees — say, wanting Bar Prep counted under Food? A per-tenant override on a fixed enum, or something already established?

9. The schema has very few database-enforced foreign keys. Given tenant separation is otherwise strict, **how should we guarantee a category's account resolves within the same `companyId`** — an FK, an application check, or an existing pattern?

---

## C. Onboarding and the second tenant

10. Bay Hill's chart came from an Orderly export. **The next tenant will not have one.** Is there an existing onboarding path for accounting configuration, or would a CoA import be net new?

11. Should there be a **default chart** for tenants without one? Bay Hill's 34 accounts look like a reasonable club template, but as a seed that is copied and edited per tenant — never shared rows.

12. Do you have visibility into **what a second tenant's chart looks like**? Bay Hill alone cannot tell us which parts of this structure generalise.

---

## D. Reporting model

13. **Does Direct Operating Cost sit below the line, or get allocated across F&B?** This decides whether prime cost is Food + Bar COGS only, or includes direct supplies. Everything else in the profitability model follows from it.

14. We think the direct-vs-indirect supply split is already latent in the data and needs no third axis:
    * `DOC + COGS` → direct, product-tied (TOGO Supplies, packaging that leaves with the product)
    * `DOC + Other Expense` → indirect, semi-fixed (Soaps & Chemicals, Linen, Restaurant Supplies)

    Does that read correctly against how FnB models cost, or is there an existing direct/indirect concept we should use instead?

15. Where should **`999900 / No Account`** map? It appears in inventory but not the chart of accounts. It needs a destination so those items do not silently drop out of rollups.

16. **Caution for validation:** Direct Operating Cost is **0.03% of counted inventory value** — $85.93, identical in all three months, a single Paper Products line. TOGO Supplies, Soaps and Restaurant Supplies all count zero. These are purchase-driven, not inventory-driven. Any DOC model should be validated against **invoice data**, not the inventory seed, or it will look like noise.

---

## E. Sequencing against the current seed

17. Two small items would ideally land before June is imported:
    * **Capture `GL Code` at import.** It is in every source row and is the evidence linking an item to its account. Without it the mapping is inferred from category names rather than stated by the source.
    * **Seed Bay Hill's chart of accounts** so account numbers attach as categories are created, rather than being backfilled.

    Are either of these small enough to do now, or should both wait for the full design?

18. May is already approved with 55 categories created without account numbers. Backfilling is a ~26-row update rather than a re-seed — **is that acceptable, or would you rather the CoA work land before the seed continues?**

19. Is there anything in the umbrella or accounts design that would **change what the importer writes**? If so it should land before the reseed; if it is purely additive reporting structure, it can follow the three months safely.
