Thought for 14s

Good question — and the answer determines what the fix should actually store. packGeometry.ts is the file that interprets it.

Ran a command, used Claude in Chrome integration
Orderly's model: three tiers, not two

Orderly represents a pack as case → inner pack → base unit, and the total is the product of all three:

total = caseQuantity × innerPackQuantity × baseUnitQuantity × unitMultiplier

The XLSX shape string encodes it as case/inner baseUnit, with the base quantity folded into the unit token. The parser pulls a leading number out of the unit itself, so 750ML becomes 750 × ML. That's why every value I saw reconciles:

Shape	Case	Inner	Base	Total
1/1 750ML (Red Breast)	1	1	750 ML	750 ML
2/12 EA (Stella)	2	12	1 EA	24 EA
1/24 EA (Coke)	1	24	1 EA	24 EA
1/10 LB (Bratwurst)	1	10	1 LB	160 OZ
6/2.5 LB (Potatoes)	6	2.5	1 LB	240 OZ

Units normalize to three dimensions — volume→ML, weight→OZ, count→EA — which is how 2/12 EA and 1/24 EA correctly compare equal. The earlier "2 → 1" display was showing tier one of three.

The #1246 change is this comment in normalizePackGeometry:

Every parsed tier must be explicit. Missing evidence is intentionally unknown rather than silently assuming a multiplier of one.

Previously missing tiers defaulted to 1. That's exactly what produced the confident-looking both packs normalize to 24 EA on top of nulls — and why my earlier sample looked clean.

FnB's schema can hold all three — it just isn't filled

This is the useful part. The catalog isn't missing columns:

Tier	Orderly field	FnB column	Populated
Case	caseQuantity	caseSize	3,052 / 3,052
Inner pack	innerPackQuantity	casePkgCount	0 / 3,052
Base qty	baseUnitQuantity	containerSize	0 / 3,052
Base unit	baseUnit	containerUnitId	0 / 3,052
Label	packSizeRaw	containerLabel	0 / 3,052

The write path fills tier one and drops tiers two and three. The columns were designed for this and sit empty.

So the fix is smaller than "add pack support" — it's populating four existing fields at the existing insert, from data already on rowPreview.

One thing that won't be fixed by this

Orderly maintains pack size as a first-class entity with its own packSizeId, and an item code can legitimately map to more than one. But there's a comment in the source worth knowing about:

XLSX exports do not expose Orderly packSize.id.

So the export gives you the shape (4/1 GALLON) but not the identity. FnB has to reconstruct pack identity from the three tiers. That's precisely why Milk-Whole is unresolvable from the workbook alone — two different packSizeIds can appear under one code, and the export doesn't carry the discriminator.

Which means even after the write path is fixed, Milk-Whole still needs a human answer. That's your asterisk, and it's inherent to the export format rather than a defect anyone can code away.
