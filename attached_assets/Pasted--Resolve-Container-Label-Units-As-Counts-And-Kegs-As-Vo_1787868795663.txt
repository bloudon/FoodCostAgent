# Resolve Container-Label Units As Counts, And Kegs As Volume

**Priority:** Should land before the Bay Hill reseed — changes what is written to the catalog
**Environment:** FnB Dev, Bay Hill CC `61971215-e3ed-49f3-8afc-6dbe1eef1fcc`
**Follows:** #1264 (measurable Orderly units), which added `GAL`, `QT`, `PT`, `Cup`, `DZ`, `KG`, `GR`

> **Numbering:** two shipped tickets are both recorded as #1256 (descriptive-code identity, and the revived #1255 parser scope). Please resolve that collision.

## What & Why

Two unit tokens remain unsupported after #1264. Both carry information the source actually states, and both currently leave otherwise-countable items permanently unresolved.

The governing principle:

> **A container label is a valid counting unit.** When the source provides multipliers, resolve the count. Never infer the container's contents.

This separates two different questions that are currently conflated:

* **Count geometry** — how many countable units are in the purchase unit. Knowable from the source.
* **Measure geometry** — what is inside each container. Not knowable, and correctly left unknown.

Failing to resolve the count because the measure is unknown means these items are invisible to counting logic indefinitely, at a value low enough that nobody notices.

---

## 1. `#10` — resolve as a count unit

A #10 can is a standard foodservice container and a legitimate counting unit. Its fill weight varies by product (pie filling vs chickpeas vs pumpkin purée), so the weight must not be inferred — but the count is stated outright.

### Evidence (May batch)

Two shapes, both meaningful:

| Shape | Rows | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `6/1 #10` | 10 | 6 cans per case |
| `1/1 #10` | 8 | sold as a single can |

Price arithmetic confirms the counting unit is the can:

| Item | Pack | Case price | Units | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chickpea/Garbanzo | `6/1 #10` | $39.13 | 6.0 | $39.13 |
| Beets | `6/1 #10` | $37.59 | 6.0 | $37.59 |
| Coconut milk | `6/1 #10` | $98.27 | **1.5** | **$24.57** |

Coconut milk is decisive: $98.27 ÷ 6 × 1.5 = $24.57 exactly. Counts are expressed in cans, and the source states cans per case.

### Requested

Treat `#10` the same way `ea` / `ct` / `count` are already treated:

```
dimension: 'each', multiplier: amount
```

`6/1 #10` then resolves to **6 EA**. This asserts only what the source states — six cans — and asserts nothing about ounces.

### Explicitly out of scope

Do **not** map `#10` to a nominal weight (e.g. 109 oz). Fill weight is product-dependent and would assert false precision on a permanent record. Weight belongs at the inventory-item level later, from a product spec or an invoice line.

### Scope

17–18 distinct items per month, ~$250–410 in value. Low value, but they remain permanently unresolved and uncountable without this.

## 2. `KEG 5.16G` — resolve as volume

The size is stated in the token: **5.16 gallons**, a sixth-barrel. Nothing is inferred. `normalizeUnit` currently extracts a numeric prefix only, so a leading word (`KEG`) causes the token to fall through to `null`.

### Corroboration

Three item names independently state the same fact:

* `SAM 0CT K **1/6BBL**`
* `STELLA ARTOIS **1/6 BBL**`
* `SIDEWARD BREWING MOON BOOTS KEG **(1/6 BBL)**`

A sixth-barrel is 5.166 US gallons.

### Requested

Extend unit parsing to find a `<number><unit>` pattern anywhere in the token, not only at the start, so `KEG 5.16G` resolves via the existing `gal` alias added in #1264 (× 3785.41 ML).

### Scope

19 rows, 9 distinct items, consistent across all three months. Worth resolving because draft costing (cost per ounce poured) is unavailable while a keg carries no volume.

### Known data anomaly — flag, do not special-case

`Miso - White` from **Mr. Green's Produce** is tagged `KEG 5.16G` in the source. Miso is not sold in kegs; this is an Orderly data-entry error. A general fix will assign it 19,532 ML — harmless for value, wrong for costing. Surface it rather than blocking on it.

## Done looks like

* `6/1 #10` resolves to 6 EA; `1/1 #10` resolves to 1 EA.
* `#10` never produces a weight or volume normalization.
* `KEG 5.16G` resolves to 19,532.72 ML (5.16 × 3785.41).
* Unsupported tokens with no recoverable information — notably bare `1/1 Case` — remain explicitly unknown and fail closed as they do today.
* Existing supported units and the fail-closed behaviour from #1264 are unchanged.
* Regressions cover: `6/1 #10` count resolution, `1/1 #10`, `KEG 5.16G` volume resolution, a token with a leading word and no number (still unknown), and `1/1 Case` (still unknown).

## Out of scope

* Inferring container contents for any container label.
* Changing `canonicalOrderlyPackKey` or identity behaviour. The identity key uses the raw normalized `baseUnit` string, so these changes must not alter it — otherwise previously-seeded items would fork.
* Correcting the `Miso - White` source row.
* Vendor classification, cross-vendor identity, or approval progress work.

## Verification

Against a fresh May import:

* Chickpea/Garbanzo, Beets, Coconut milk show a normalized total in **EA**, not "Not confirmed".
* Coconut milk's 1.5 units still values at $24.57.
* The nine keg items show a normalized volume.
* `1/1 Case` rows (e.g. `GUINNESS 4PK`, `GUINES DRAUGHT`) still read as unknown geometry.
* May still reconciles: 5,358 rows, 0 excluded, imported total $254,286.67.
