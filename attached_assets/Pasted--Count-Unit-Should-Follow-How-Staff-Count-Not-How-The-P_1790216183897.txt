# Count Unit Should Follow How Staff Count, Not How The Pack Is Weighed

**Context:** Bay Hill count-setup review, 200 items flagged for pack verification
**Scope:** How FnB derives an item's canonical count unit from Orderly pack notation
**Not a bug in one item — a modelling rule that affects the whole catalog**

---

## The principle

**An item's canonical unit should be the thing a person picks up when counting.**

For a bag of chips that is a bag. For a case of broccoli it is a pound. The unit is a property of how the product is handled and sold, not of what the pack notation happens to state.

Weight is only the right unit when the product is genuinely weighed or portioned by weight.

---

## The worked example

`Asst Lays Chips`, retained packs `1/30 EA | 1/60 EA`, canonical unit `ea`.

Research confirms these are not competing case sizes: **Frito-Lay's 60-count foodservice case is two 30-count inner boxes.** Vendor listings describe it as "(2) 30-count variety boxes" and "30 bags per tray, 2 trays per case, giving a stock of 60 individual packs."

So both numbers are real — 60 is the master case, 30 is the inner box. Staff have confirmed counting by the 60-bag case.

**Crucially, the case is not a fixed weight.** Its contents mix 1.5 oz Lay's, 2 oz Cheetos, 1.75 oz Doritos and 1.125 oz Baked Lay's. There is no meaningful "ounces per case."

The club sells a member **one bag** with a sandwich. The cost that matters is `case price ÷ 60`. Any attempt to express this item in ounces would be both impossible to derive and useless once derived.

`ea` is correct here, and `1/60 EA` is the current standard.

---

## Where the model currently goes wrong

The canonical unit appears to be derived from the **pack notation's unit token** rather than from the nature of the product. When a source pack says `12/32 OZ`, the item becomes ounce-based — even when what staff actually handle is a tub, a tray, or a sleeve.

**19 items in this worklist are on `oz` or `ml` while sitting in categories that are discrete by nature:**

| Item | Category | Unit | Retained packs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chocolate Chip Cookie Dough | Vendor Prepared Items | oz | `1/80 EA \| 80/4.5 OZ` |
| Vegetable Blends/Medley's | Vendor Prepared Items | oz | `12/32 OZ \| 6/4 LB` |
| Veggie Burger | Vendor Prepared Items | oz | `29/5.5 OZ \| 32/6 OZ` |
| Roasted Potatoes | Vendor Prepared Items | oz | `5/3 LB \| 6/2.5 LB` |
| Muffin | Bread | oz | `24/4 OZ \| 96/2.25 OZ` |
| San Pellegrino | Non-Alcoholic Beverages | ml | `1/12 EA \| 15/1 750ML` |
| Lemonade | Non-Alcoholic Beverages | oz | `15/14 OZ \| 15/2 GAL` |
| Tofu - Firm | Dry Goods | oz | `12/16 OZ \| 12/19 OZ` |

`Chocolate Chip Cookie Dough` is the clearest case: one retained pack literally says `1/80 EA` — 80 pucks — while the other says `80/4.5 OZ`, which is the same 80 pucks *described by weight*. The item is 80 discrete portions. It is currently `oz`.

`Muffin` at `24/4 OZ | 96/2.25 OZ` is 24 muffins or 96 muffins. Nobody weighs a muffin.

`San Pellegrino` at `1/12 EA | 15/1 750ML` is bottles, counted as bottles.

---

## Requested rule

**Derive the canonical unit from what is counted, with the pack notation as evidence rather than as the answer.**

1. **When any retained pack for an item is expressed in `EA`, the item is discrete.** Canonical unit is `ea`; the other pack's weight is the *size of one unit*, not the counting dimension. Cookie Dough `1/80 EA | 80/4.5 OZ` → 80 ea, each 4.5 oz.

2. **When a pack states a count of identically-sized containers** — `12/32 OZ`, `24/4 OZ`, `15/1 750ML` — the countable unit is the container. Record both: `ea` for counting, container size for costing and recipes.

3. **Weight or volume is canonical only when the product is genuinely bulk** — meat by the pound, oil by the litre, flour by the kilo. Broccoli at `1/20 POUND` is correctly weight-based.

4. **Never average a weight across a mixed case.** Where a case contains different-sized items (variety packs), weight is not derivable and must not be synthesised.

---

## Why this matters beyond tidiness

**Counting.** Staff count what they can see. Asking for ounces of muffins produces a guess or a blank.

**Costing.** Cost per bag, per muffin, per burger is the figure a chef uses. Cost per ounce of a variety pack is meaningless.

**Recipes.** A recipe calls for one burger patty or two ounces of cheese. The unit must match how the ingredient is consumed, and those are different answers for different items.

**Variance.** A wrong unit does not produce a small error; it produces a nonsensical one. An item counted in bags but valued per ounce will be wrong by whatever the bag weight happens to be.

---

## Done looks like

* Canonical unit derives from countability, not from the first unit token found in pack notation.
* Any item with an `EA` pack among its retained packs defaults to `ea`, with weight retained as unit size.
* A pack of the form `N/M <weight or volume unit>` where N > 1 is recognised as N countable containers of M.
* Items in inherently discrete categories — Vendor Prepared Items, Bread, Paper Products, TOGO Supplies — are not assigned weight units without explicit evidence that they are weighed.
* Mixed-content cases are never assigned a synthetic average weight.
* The 19 items listed above are re-evaluated under the corrected rule.
* Existing approved inventory values are unchanged — this affects unit and costing derivation, not imported value.

---

## Out of scope

* Changing approved May, June or July batch values.
* Re-running any import.
* Resolving the 123 `1/1 Case` items, whose contents are genuinely unknown at source and must come from staff or a packing slip.
