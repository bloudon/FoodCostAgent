# Base Category Set And Food/Beverage Split — All Tiers

**Type:** Product foundation
**Applies to:** Every tenant. The Food/Beverage split is the product, not an enterprise feature.
**Related:** CoA import (enterprise only) · #1275 F&B reporting umbrellas

---

## What & Why

FnB should show Food vs Beverage for every tenant, at every tier. It is the product's core distinction and its name.

Today that split does not exist. Reporting stops at category level, and nothing groups Wine + Beer + Liquor into Beverage.

The split must **not** depend on a chart of accounts. Enterprise tenants have one; a single-location restaurant does not, and should not have to load a chart before seeing their beverage spend.

**Therefore the umbrella lives on the category, not the account.** Categories exist for every tenant. Accounts exist only for enterprise tenants, and can *populate* the umbrella rather than being required for it.

---

## The umbrella

A fixed platform enum on every category:

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `Food` | Food cost of sales |
| `Beverage` | Beverage cost of sales |
| `Other` | Supplies and non-COGS operating items |

Fixed, not free text. Tenant-comparable classification is the point; free-form values make cross-tenant reporting impossible and are expensive to retrofit.

Whether a fourth value (`Direct Operating Cost`) is separated from `Other` is deferred to **#1275**. `Other` is sufficient for the first release.

---

## Base category set — non-enterprise tenants

Ten categories, seeded for new tenants who are not importing an existing structure. Each carries its umbrella by definition, so a small operator gets the Food/Beverage split with **zero configuration**.

| # | Category | Umbrella |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Produce | Food |
| 2 | Meat & Poultry | Food |
| 3 | Seafood | Food |
| 4 | Dairy | Food |
| 5 | Dry Goods | Food |
| 6 | Beer | Beverage |
| 7 | Wine | Beverage |
| 8 | Liquor | Beverage |
| 9 | Non-Alcoholic Beverages | Beverage |
| 10 | Supplies | Other |

### Design notes

**Beer, Wine and Liquor stay separate** even at ten slots. That is where beverage margin lives, and it is the split that ties to revenue accounts later for enterprise tenants. Food detail was sacrificed instead — Bread and Pastry fold into Dry Goods.

**`Non-Alcoholic Beverages` is the one genuinely contestable default.** It is $9,194 at Bay Hill and Orderly classifies it **Food** — sodas and juices bought on the food side — but much of it is poured at the bar as mixers. Defaulting it to `Beverage` matches user expectation; it must be overridable, and it is the category most likely to be moved.

**Tenants may add categories beyond the ten.** Anything added needs an umbrella — see the default-by-name rule below.

---

## Default-by-name rule

Category names are highly predictable. Any category not in the base set — created by a tenant, or arriving via an import — should be **pre-filled** with an umbrella and presented for confirmation, never presented blank.

Suggested matching, case-insensitive, on substring:

| Contains | Umbrella |
|---|---|
| wine, beer, liquor, spirit, alcohol, bar, cocktail, mixer | Beverage |
| soda, juice, coffee, tea, non-alc | Beverage |
| produce, meat, poultry, seafood, dairy, bread, pastry, dry, grocer, prep, food | Food |
| paper, chemical, soap, supply, supplies, togo, to-go, linen, smallware | Other |
| *no match* | Unassigned — requires confirmation |

Validated against Bay Hill's 26 imported categories: 25 match unambiguously. The exception is `No Account`, the Orderly placeholder, which maps to Unassigned.

**Present as review-and-confirm, not assign-from-blank.** The common case should be zero decisions.

## Tier boundaries

| Capability | Non-enterprise | Enterprise |
|---|---|---|
| Food / Beverage / Other split | ✅ | ✅ |
| Base 10 categories seeded | ✅ | superseded by import |
| Custom categories with default-by-name | ✅ | ✅ |
| Chart of accounts | ❌ | ✅ |
| Category → GL account mapping | ❌ | ✅ |
| Umbrella pre-filled from account `Operational Type` | n/a | ✅ |

**A tenant must be able to run FnB with `accountingAccountId` null on every category.** That is already how the schema behaves and must not regress.

For enterprise tenants, the chart of accounts becomes a *source* for the umbrella rather than a prerequisite: Bay Hill's accounts carry `Operational Type` (15 Food, 11 Bar, 7 Direct Operating Cost, 1 Other), which can pre-fill each linked category's umbrella. Note the mapping is Bar → `Beverage`.

---

## Done looks like

* Every category has an umbrella value from the fixed enum, or is explicitly Unassigned.
* New non-enterprise tenants are seeded with the ten base categories, each carrying its umbrella; no configuration required to see the split.
* Categories created outside the base set are pre-filled by the name rule and presented for confirmation.
* Bay Hill's 26 existing categories can be assigned umbrellas — 25 by name rule, `No Account` explicitly.
* Inventory value and purchase spend can be reported grouped by Food / Beverage / Other.
* The split works with no chart of accounts present.
* For enterprise tenants, an account's `Operational Type` can pre-fill the umbrella on categories linked to it.
* Changing a category's umbrella is audited.

## Out of scope

* Separating `Direct Operating Cost` from `Other` — #1275
* Revenue attribution, menu items, or recipe-based cost allocation
* Cross-tenant benchmarking
* Chart of accounts import — enterprise, separate ticket
* Unassigned-item worklist

## Important framing for the UI

This split is **purchase-side**: what was bought and held, by category. It is *not* beverage cost of sales against beverage revenue.

Bay Hill illustrates the gap. `Non-Alcoholic Beverages` at $9,194 is purchased as Food but substantially consumed at the bar as mixers, while `Bar Prep` is only $600. True Food vs Beverage margin requires POS revenue and recipe-level allocation, neither of which exists yet.

**Label accordingly** — "Beverage spend" or "Beverage inventory value", not "Beverage cost of sales" — so nobody reads a margin off it that it cannot support.
