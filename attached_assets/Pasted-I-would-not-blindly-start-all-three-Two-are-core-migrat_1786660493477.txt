I would **not blindly start all three**. Two are core migration-integrity checks; one risks turning into premature UI work.

My PM call:

### 1. Duplicate invoice IDs in one upload — **DO NOW**

This is directly tied to #1098’s idempotency contract.

We already said:

> identical source identity → no duplicate
> changed record with same source identity → conflict
> never silently overwrite

So this should be proven before we rely on historical invoice import.

Direction:

* duplicate source invoice IDs in the same batch must not inflate history/completeness counts
* exact duplicates should collapse/no-op safely
* conflicting duplicates with the same source identity should be surfaced as conflicts
* DB uniqueness remains the final protection

### 2. Missing historical months before cutover — **KEEP, BUT REFRAME**

The capability is necessary, but I would not make this a “manager UI” project yet.

Rename it conceptually to:

> **Report invoice-history month coverage before cutover**

The migration completeness report should tell us:

```text
Required window: Aug 2025 – Jul 2026

Aug 2025   Complete
Sep 2025   Complete
Oct 2025   Missing
Nov 2025   Complete
...
Jul 2026   Complete

11 / 12 required months complete
CUTOVER NOT READY
```

That belongs with the **adapter/dry-run completeness gate**.

A fancy manager-facing screen can wait. We need the evidence first.

### 3. Year and cutover boundaries — **DO NOW**

This is also core #1098 correctness.

I want automated proof around:

* Dec 31 → Jan 1
* cutover on first day of month
* cutover mid-month
* “12 complete calendar months immediately before cutover”
* leap-year February where applicable
* no accidental inclusion of the partial cutover month
* date-only semantics not shifting because of timezone conversion

For example, if cutover is August 15, 2026:

> Required beta history = August 1, 2025 through July 31, 2026.

Not:

> August 15, 2025 through August 14, 2026.

That distinction matters.
