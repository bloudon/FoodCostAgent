I would **not approve #1191 as written yet.** The problem is real, and we should fix it, but the proposed uniqueness key is currently too aggressive for the purchasing model we just established.

The dangerous assumption is:

> `(vendor_id, inventory_item_id, vendor_sku)` = one real vendor product

That is only safe if **one vendor SKU can never legitimately represent more than one purchase pack/geometry for the same inventory item**. We have not proven that yet. And treating `NULL SKU` as `''` makes it even riskier.

We just established in #1178 that the vendor product is the purchasing identity and **Pack Size is material evidence**. I don't want #1191 to erase legitimate pack variants while cleaning artifacts created by the inventory duplicate remediation.

### PM decision: HOLD APPLY, approve a short classifier first

The 6,207 excess rows are almost certainly heavily polluted by the old duplicate-item problem, so this is worth doing. But Step 1 should begin by separating **obvious duplicates** from **potentially distinct vendor products**.

### The part I strongly disagree with

This sentence in Step 1:

> “merge any non-conflicting field values worth keeping (**latest price/pack geometry**)”

I would remove **pack geometry** from that completely.

Pack geometry helps define the vendor product. It isn't merely metadata we can resolve by picking the newest version. If two rows have materially different packs, that is evidence they may **not be duplicates at all**.

Price is different: dated price records can all survive after being repointed to one proven vendor product. We don't need to throw historical prices away to consolidate duplicate catalog identities.

### The NULL SKU constraint is also too broad

This proposed constraint:

```sql
(vendor_id, inventory_item_id, coalesce(vendor_sku, ''))
```

would assert:

> A vendor can have only one SKU-less purchasing product for an inventory item.

We haven't proven that, and beverage/produce catalog data is exactly where I wouldn't make that assumption.

A safer eventual model may be something like:

* reliable non-null SKU → strong vendor/SKU uniqueness within the appropriate company/source scope;
* no SKU → external product identity or pack identity required before automatic consolidation;
* truly ambiguous SKU-less products remain separate/reviewable.

But I want the classifier numbers before choosing that schema.

### Harvill's makes this worth doing

The Harvill's result—**354 catalog rows for only 118 apparent item/SKU pairs**—is a strong signal that there is real cleanup available. And cleaning it should dramatically improve the held-line resolution problem we just found.

But this is another identity remediation. We've learned the lesson already:

> **Prove equivalence first. Merge second. Add the database invariant last.**

So: **yes to #1191, but turn Step 1 into a read-only classification gate before Builder gets permission to delete anything.**
