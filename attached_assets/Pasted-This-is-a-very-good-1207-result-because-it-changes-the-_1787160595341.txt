This is a very good #1207 result because it changes the diagnosis in an important way.

The original theory was:

> “FnB only keeps one vendor per ingredient.”

The evidence now says that is **not** the architecture bug.

The actual issue is narrower:

> **Approved earlier batches seeded some vendor relationships, but later source batches introduced additional legitimate vendor/SKU relationships that were never adopted into the persisted vendor catalog.**

That is much better news. It means our underlying vendor-product model is capable of representing multiple suppliers correctly; we have a **cross-batch adoption gap**, not a one-vendor-per-item data-model failure.

## PM disposition

**#1207 ACCEPTED / CLOSED.**

And I would split the next work into two independent tracks:

1. **#1208 — fix the cross-shopping identity bug. GO.**
2. **New vendor-product adoption/backfill task — GO for design/build in Dev, using the safely matched 136 missing relationships.**

I would not do a full catalog rebuild based on this evidence. We have a bounded, authoritative missing population: **136 relationships across 12 vendors**. That argues strongly for a targeted adoption/backfill after fixing the importer behavior that caused the gap.

### #1208 is a real bug, not optional cleanup

This finding:

> cross-shopping selects “current” comparison price by `vendorId` instead of `vendorItemId`

needs to be fixed **before we restore the missing relationships**.

Otherwise adding legitimate same-vendor products such as:

* Harvill `SPRBR5` — 5 lb
* Harvill `SPRBR` — 25 lb

could make the comparison screen more wrong, not less.

Routing itself sounds correctly vendor-item-centric already. Price history is correctly vendor-item-centric too. So #1208 should remain very small:

```md
## PM Decision — #1208

APPROVED.

Fix bulk cross-shopping so every price/comparison decision remains keyed to the exact `vendor_item_id`.

Do not identify a selected product by `vendorId` once multiple vendor products from the same vendor are present.

Required regression:

One canonical ingredient
+ same vendor
+ two legitimate vendor_items
+ different SKU/pack
+ different price histories

Prove:

- each row displays the price belonging to its own vendor_item
- price sorting does not cause one SKU to inherit another SKU's price
- selected comparison identity remains vendor_item-specific
- routing/PO selection preserves the exact chosen vendor_item_id

No routing rewrite.
No catalog mutation.
```

### Then repair the actual seeding gap

### One especially important finding

You noted:

> current active Bay Hill vendor-product rows have **zero external source-mapping records**.

I would make fixing that part of the adoption/backfill contract.

We now know that vendor-product identity matters across:

* invoice resolution
* price history
* cross-shopping
* routing

So the restored relationships should not just be rows that happen to have the right SKU. They should carry **durable Orderly provenance**, ideally including `packSize.id` wherever we have it.

That gives us something authoritative to resolve against later instead of repeatedly reconstructing identity from names/SKUs.

Keep #1205 parked.

Those residual holds will finally tell us the population the modal truly needs to solve:
